# 08 — Phase 2 Architecture Test

*The proposed architecture (docs 01–07) tested against ST-1 to ST-5 and the supporting tests from the Phase 2 brief.*

**Verdicts:**

- **PASS**: the architecture meets the test as designed.
- **CONDITIONAL**: it meets the test if the named conditions hold.
- **FAIL**: it does not meet the test.

`TIMETABLE DEPENDENT` marks parts that cannot be confirmed without the TAD programme.

---

## Primary tests

### ST-1 — Does the recruit reapply their priority with outside evidence before the next major review?

**Verdict: PASS by design; frequency TIMETABLE DEPENDENT.**

| Review | Reapplication with outside evidence before it |
|---|---|
| First priority (Day 2) | Day 1 insight reapplied in the Day 2 pressure task, observed by a buddy (02, 2C) |
| Ex One review | At least one buddy checkpoint between the two days and Ex One (03 §3). **TIMETABLE DEPENDENT** |
| Ex Two review | At least one buddy checkpoint between Ex One and Ex Two. **TIMETABLE DEPENDENT** |
| March-out | Ex Two feedback on LDP-1; Section Commander question before march-out |

**Condition:** checkpoints happen. Mitigation: attach to an existing routine, with a Section Commander reminder (03 §7).

### ST-2 — Does Identity vs Reputation evidence arrive well before Exercise One?

**Verdict: PASS.**

On Day 1, in week 1, each recruit writes an identity statement, is observed in a real section task, and receives peer and Section Commander feedback. They then compare intention with impact (02, 1B–1F). Day 2 adds a second piece of evidence. This happens before Ex One by definition (OC-1).

### ST-3 — Does every workbook activity prepare for, help perform through, or help extract development from an experience?

**Verdict: PASS, with two justified exceptions.**

Every retained activity was tested (06 §2). Activities that failed have moved to reference or prompt, or are proposed for removal. Topic treatments remain PROVISIONAL under the revised content rule (06 §1.1).

| Justified exception | Reason |
|---|---|
| Mental-health pathway | Duty of care |
| Doctrinal reference content | Doctrine requires it to be available |

## Additional tests

### ST-4 — Could a Corporal realistically sustain this while performing their normal Section Commander role?

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL.**

| For | Against / conditions |
|---|---|
| The sustaining minimum is five behaviours, all attached to existing moments, with no paperwork (04 §2) | **Depends on Section Commander buy-in and understanding.** This is the purpose of T3 (T1, T2) |
| The core behaviour (one question) takes seconds and is normal section leadership | **Facilitating two feedback rounds** is real effort. Acceptable only if it replaces existing post-exercise activity (confirm Q12) |
| The language reinforcement costs nothing extra | **Coach vs assessor tension** (Q15) may reduce effectiveness |
| | Section size and Section Commander turnover are unknown |

**Conditions to pass:** T3 builds understanding, not just delivery. The Ex One/Two rounds replace rather than add. Psychological safety is addressed. The five behaviours pass the support-vs-administer test (04 §2.1). **Validation:** Section Commander conversations SC-1 to SC-8 (`10`).

### ST-5 — Is there a compelling reason for the recruit to use the development tool after Day 2?

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL. This is the weakest test.**

| For | Against / conditions |
|---|---|
| A social trigger: the Section Commander asks about the priority (05 §1.2) | It works only if Section Commanders actually ask (links to ST-4) |
| Performance value: tools and priority before hard training; a debrief after | **The causes of current non-use are hypotheses (H1–H7), not findings** |
| Exercise reviews need evidence that is already in Part 2 | Format deliberately unresolved. A large book may repeat the locker problem (H3), and so might a new small item recruits must remember to carry |
| Visible progress at march-out | Recruits' time and energy in TAD are very limited |
| Very low recording demand (one line per checkpoint) | |

**Conditions to pass:** check the hypotheses with recruits and Section Commanders. Place Part 2 wherever recruits actually have access (format unresolved; FR-1 to FR-8 in 05). Ensure the Section Commander question actually happens. Ideally pilot with one intake (09). **Validation:** recruit conversations R-1 to R-8 (`10`).

## Overall assessment

The architecture meets the three primary tests by design. Its two weakest points are both **human**, not structural:

1. whether Section Commanders take on the coaching role (ST-4);
2. whether recruits find the tool worth using (ST-5).

Neither can be settled by more design work. Both need contact with real Section Commanders and recruits. A1 is therefore **provisionally accepted, pending lightweight user validation** (`10`), with a pilot intake recommended once Phase 3 designs are ready.
