# 04 — Section Commander Role

*The minimum Section Commander (Cpl) behaviours that keep Lead Self development going after Day 2, and what the NZALC Train the Trainer (T3) package must give Section Commanders so they can do this confidently and consistently.*

**Design goal:** maximum developmental value for minimum additional burden. Section Commanders are coaches and feedback sources, **not workbook administrators** (08 §1.0).

---

## 1. The Section Commander has three jobs in this system

| Job | When | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| **Deliver** the two Lead Self days | Week 1 | Formal, scripted, NZALC-designed |
| **Sustain** the loop through normal section leadership | Rest of TAD | Informal, embedded, seconds to minutes |
| **Facilitate** the major evidence points | Ex One, Ex Two (and march-out) | Structured, short, NZALC-designed |

---

## 2. Candidate sustaining behaviours, tested

The brief asked whether each candidate mechanism is actually required. Each is tested for developmental value against burden.

| Mechanism | Developmental value | Burden | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Know each recruit's priority** | High. It makes every other behaviour possible | Very low: recall one line per recruit in the section (or keep a section list) | **Essential** |
| **Ask one coaching question at natural moments** ("You said X. How did you go today?") | **Very high.** It is the cue, the feedback prompt and the reflection prompt in one | Seconds. Fits after an AAR, an inspection, an activity, or at the end-of-day brief | **Essential** |
| **Facilitate the Ex One / Ex Two feedback rounds** | High. These are the major evidence points (DR-3) | Moderate, but it replaces existing post-exercise activity rather than adding to it (confirm Q12) | **Essential** |
| **Give one observation per recruit per major cycle** | High. It is the only non-peer evidence; it calibrates peer feedback | Low–moderate: one DEB/DESC-style observation per recruit, verbal | **Essential** |
| **Reinforce the behavioural language** (Above / Below the Line, Value Adds, zones) in normal correction and praise | High. It makes Lead Self part of how the section is led rather than a separate subject | Near zero. It changes words, not workload | **Strongly recommended** |
| **Prompt a pressure debrief after a hard day** ("What got in your way today? What will you do next time?") | Medium–high | Low. Can be the end-of-day section brief | **Recommended, situational** |
| **Model development openly** (share own development point) | High for psychological safety | Near zero | **Recommended** |
| **Check workbooks / sign pages / mark entries** | Low. It turns development into compliance | Moderate–high | **Excluded** |
| **Run additional Lead Self lessons** | Low marginal value | High | **Excluded** unless clearly necessary (OC-3) |
| **Keep formal records of coaching conversations** | Low for development; unclear purpose | High | **Excluded** (unless D1/16 assessment requires something) |

### The Section Commander minimum, in one line

> **Know each recruit's priority. Ask about it at natural moments. Give one honest observation at each exercise. Run the two exercise feedback rounds. Use the language.**

---

## 3. Coaching question bank (illustrative; finalised in Phase 3)

Short and open. Usable in passing.

| Purpose | Question |
|---|---|
| Check the priority | "What's your priority at the moment?" |
| Prompt evidence | "When did that come up today? What did you do?" |
| Prompt reflection | "How did it land with the section?" / "What got in the way?" |
| Prompt adapt | "What will you do differently next time?" |
| Prompt reapply | "When's your next chance to try it?" |
| Pressure lens | "Which zone were you in? Could you still make decisions and notice others?" |
| Above / Below the Line | "Was that above or below the line? What would above look like?" |
| Strength | "That was a good example of [Value Add]. What made it work?" |

---

## 4. Where Section Commander behaviours attach (candidate moments)

| Existing moment | Section Commander behaviour |
|---|---|
| End-of-day section brief | One or two priority questions; a reminder for buddy check-ins |
| Post-activity AAR | Use the behavioural language; one question to a recruit whose priority was relevant |
| Inspections, routine correction | Above / Below the Line language |
| Before an exercise | "Know your priority. Know your tool." (PREPARE) |
| After an exercise | Facilitate the feedback round (RECOVER) |
| Before march-out | Prompt: "What evidence shows you've changed?" |

**All placements are TIMETABLE DEPENDENT.** The design principle is fixed: use moments that already exist.

---

## 5. Risks specific to the Section Commander role

| Risk | Why it matters | Design response |
|---|---|---|
| **Coach vs assessor tension.** The Section Commander may also assess and discipline recruits (Q15) | Recruits may give "safe" answers or pick safe priorities if development feels graded | Separate development conversations from assessment in language and intent; Section Commanders model their own development; T3 covers psychological safety; D1/16 decides what, if anything, is assessed |
| **Variability across sections** | Some Section Commanders will do this well, some won't | Keep the minimum small; give them scripts and question cards; T3 builds understanding, not just lesson delivery |
| **Section Commander overload** | TAD is busy for staff too | Only five essential behaviours; no paperwork; attach to existing moments |
| **Section Commander's own feedback skill** | Feedback quality determines evidence quality | T3 includes giving DEB/DESC feedback, practised |
| **Disclosures and wellbeing** | Reflection under pressure can surface real issues | T3 covers recognising concerns and the referral pathway; mental-health content stays duty of care (D10/13) |
| **Turnover / posting of Section Commanders mid-course** | Continuity breaks | The recruit's priority and evidence live with the recruit (workbook), so a new Section Commander can pick up with one question. **TAD to confirm frequency** |

## 6. What the NZALC T3 package must give Section Commanders

This is the **second product** (L22). Phase 2 defines what it must achieve, not its content or duration (Q14).

| # | T3 must give Section Commanders… | Why |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Understanding of the system**: why the two days exist, the recruit loop, the three structures, and their own role as coach | Section Commanders who understand the system adapt well. Those who only have lesson plans revert to delivering content (the project lead's point) |
| T2 | **Experience of the loop themselves**: an identity statement, an observed task, feedback, one priority of their own | They coach what they have done. It also gives them a development point to model |
| T3 | **Confidence to deliver the two days**: scripts, activity instructions, observation prompts, debrief questions, timings guidance | Delivered by Corporals, not facilitators (02, P3) |
| T4 | **Feedback skill**: give one specific DEB/DESC observation; model receiving feedback; manage a section feedback round | Evidence quality and psychological safety |
| T5 | **Coaching micro-skill**: the question bank; asking without lecturing; listening for a behaviour, not a trait | The core sustaining behaviour |
| T6 | **Running the pressure task safely** and framing it with Prepare → Perform → Recover | Day 2, block 2C |
| T7 | **Facilitating the Ex One / Ex Two feedback rounds** | Major evidence points |
| T8 | **What not to do**: don't check workbooks, don't grade reflections, don't write the LDP for the recruit | Avoids turning development into administration |
| T9 | **Recognising wellbeing concerns and the referral pathway** | Duty of care |
| T10 | **Job aids**: one-page Section Commander card (minimum behaviours + question bank); section priority list template (optional, for their own use) | Consistency under pressure of time |
