# 05 — Workbook Role and Architecture

*Recruits are meant to keep and use the workbook through TAD. In practice they typically do not (OC-6). This document treats that as a design failure to solve. It defines what the workbook is for, and what belongs in Part 1 versus Part 2. **No pages are designed.***

---

## 1. Why would a recruit reopen it after Day 2?

"Because they are told to" is not an answer. Instructed use produces compliance at best and fiction at worst.

### 1.1 Why it currently isn't reopened: hypotheses

These are **audit-based hypotheses, not findings**. No recruit or Section Commander has been asked. They should be checked before Phase 3 commits to a format (09, P3-1).

| # | Hypothesis | Phase 1 evidence that supports it |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | **No occasion.** Nothing in TAD creates a moment where opening it helps | The workbook is organised by lesson. After the lessons end, it has no hook into training (Phase 1 doc 03) |
| H2 | **Looks finished.** Once the lessons are done, it reads as a completed classroom book | 67 of 84 numbered pages sit in the topic chapters before Ch 13, which is the only forward-looking part |
| H3 | **Too big and too formal to carry.** An A4 bound workbook belongs in a locker, not a pocket | 91 pages; print format |
| H4 | **Feels like admin.** Repetitive forms signal paperwork, not development | 27 Keep/Stop/Start instances; 22 identical reflection pages |
| H5 | **No audience.** Nobody asks about what's in it, so there is no social reason to keep it current | No Section Commander role defined in the current workbook |
| H6 | **No payoff.** It doesn't visibly help with what recruits care about: getting through, performing on exercise, standing in the section | Pressure tools sit at the back (Ch 12) |
| H7 | **Plans are never followed up**, so recruits learn that writing a plan is the end of it | LDP-1 is never reviewed (Phase 1 doc 06, W5) |

### 1.2 Genuine reasons a recruit would reopen it

| Reason | When | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Someone asks.** Their Section Commander asks about their priority, so they want to know what they said and have something to show | Any day | Priority must be instantly findable; evidence lines must be quick to read (social trigger, H5) |
| **It helps them perform.** Before an exercise or a hard day, a quick reminder of priority and tool | PREPARE | Part 2 opens with the priority and a small set of tools (H6) |
| **It helps after a hard day.** A two-minute debrief makes sense of something that went wrong | RECOVER | A short, reusable pressure debrief prompt (H6) |
| **The feedback round needs it.** At Ex One and Ex Two they need their evidence | Major reviews | Evidence lines feed the exercise review directly (H1) |
| **They can see progress.** Seeing their identity statement, first priority and later evidence side by side is motivating | Exercises, march-out | A visible thread: start → now (H2, H7) |
| **It's small enough to have on them** | Always | Compact Part 2 (H3) |

**Design principle:** the workbook earns reopening by being **useful at a moment that already matters to the recruit**. It is not the reason for the moment.

## 2. The role of the workbook in the system

| The workbook IS | The workbook IS NOT |
|---|---|
| The recruit's development tool | A record for staff to check |
| The **evidence thread**: identity → feedback → priority → reapply → review → march-out | A diary or a log of every day |
| A cue (the priority is visible) | The main driver of development. Conversations are |
| A small reference of tools and language | A textbook of every model |
| Owned and kept by the recruit | Assessed (unless D1/16 requires part of it) |

It should progressively become **less of a classroom workbook and more of a simple personal development tool** (08 §1.2).

## 3. Part 1 and Part 2

### PART 2 — MY LEAD SELF DEVELOPMENT

**Used during:** the rest of TAD, driven by the recruit loop.
**Proposed nature:** a **compact field development tool**, closer to a pocket journal than a workbook.

| Contains (function, not pages) | Used at | Recorded? |
|---|---|---|
| **My priority**: "When ___, I will ___", Value Add, date set. Always the first thing seen | Everyday (cue) | Updated when the priority changes |
| **My tools**: the small pressure toolkit on one panel | PREPARE / PERFORM | No |
| **Two-minute debrief prompt** (what happened, what I did, what worked, what next) | After hard days (optional) | Optional |
| **Evidence lines**: one line per checkpoint (date, what happened, keep/adjust) | Checkpoints (TIMETABLE DEPENDENT) | One line |
| **Exercise One review**: self-assessment, peer and Section Commander feedback summary, review of first priority | Ex One | Yes |
| **LDP-1** (recruit-owned): priority, Value Add, Behaviour Statement, evidence, strategy, situations, "how will I know?" | After Ex One | Yes |
| **Exercise Two review**: including "did LDP-1 work?" | Ex Two | Yes |
| **LDP-2 / march-out**: identity statement revisited; section commitment revisited; priority for next unit | Before march-out | Yes |

**Separate from both parts:** peer feedback sheets for the exercise rounds (so tearing them out doesn't destroy workbook pages; Phase 1 finding, D12).

## 4. Format options for Part 2 (decision for Phase 3)

| Option | Description | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Part 2 at the back of one book** | One printed workbook, two parts | Simplest to print and issue | Inherits H2 and H3: big, classroom-feeling, stays in the locker |
| **B. Separate pocket booklet** | Part 1 is the classroom book; Part 2 is a small booklet issued on Day 2 | Portable; signals a new purpose; fits a pocket | Two items to print and manage; could be lost |
| **C. Card + exercise pages** | A priority/tools card carried daily; exercise review and LDP pages kept in Part 1 | Minimal daily carry; strong cue | Evidence thread split across items |
| **D. Insert into an existing recruit notebook** | Part 2 as pages or a sleeve in something recruits already carry | Rides existing habit | Depends on what recruits carry; **TAD to confirm** |
| **E. Digital** | Phone or app | Always on hand | Phone access in TAD is likely restricted; equity; **not recommended without TAD confirmation** |

**Leaning:** **B or D**, both tested against H1–H7. The choice depends on the hypothesis check (09, P3-1) and on what recruits actually carry (TAD).

---

## 5. Test against the brief's question

> *Why would a recruit voluntarily reopen this after Day 2?*

**Answer this architecture gives:**

- Because their Section Commander will ask about their priority, and the answer is on the first page.
- Because it holds the two tools that help before and after hard training.
- Because at each exercise their evidence is needed and is already there.
- Because by march-out it shows them, in their own words and others', how they've changed.

**Honest caveat.** This answer depends on the Section Commander actually asking (04) and on the format being carryable. Neither is guaranteed. ST-5 (08) is therefore marked **conditional**.
