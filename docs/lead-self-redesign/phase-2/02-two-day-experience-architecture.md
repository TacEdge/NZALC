# 02 — Two-Day Experience Architecture

*A conceptual architecture for the two Section Commander-delivered Lead Self days in Week 1, before Ex One. It is built from the recruit experience and outcomes (01), not from existing chapters.*

**This is not a timetable or lesson plan.** Blocks are in logical order. Durations are not set. Relative weight (light / medium / heavy) is shown only to signal where the time should go. Day titles are working titles.

---

## 1. Design principles for these two days

| # | Principle | Why |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Experience before explanation.** Each concept is introduced as close as possible to an experience that makes it real, ideally just after it | Recruits are 18, in week one, and tired. Concepts with nothing attached to them don't last |
| P2 | **The loop turns once inside the two days.** Day 1 produces feedback and an insight; Day 2 tests that insight under pressure, with evidence | Recruits leave having *done* the method (01, E5) |
| P3 | **Deliverable by a Corporal.** Short concept inputs, clear activity instructions, observation prompts and debrief questions. No reliance on advanced facilitation skill | Delivered by Section Commanders, not NZALC instructors (OC-2) |
| P4 | **The section is the unit.** Recruits work with their real section and their real Section Commander | Feedback and commitments carry straight into the rest of TAD. The relationships the loop depends on start here |
| P5 | **No standalone lesson by default.** Topics get deliberate instruction only where recruits need knowledge, a model or a tool before they can practise. Otherwise they show up as an activity, debrief question, lens or reference (06 §1.1) | Protects the two days from cramming (risk R4) without starving recruits of models they need |
| P6 | **Low stakes, high specificity.** Feedback is about one observed behaviour, not about the person | Builds the habit safely in week one |
| P7 | **Every block produces something the recruit carries forward**, or prepares for something that does | ST-3 |

**Mapping to the NZALC method.** Across the two days, LEARN → PRACTICE → PRESSURE → APPLY → REINFORCE runs as follows. Day 1 is mostly LEARN and PRACTICE (feedback). Day 2 adds PRESSURE and APPLY (reapply under pressure). The close of Day 2 begins REINFORCE (handover).

## 2. Day 1 — Understand Me and My Impact

*Recruit question: Who am I? How do I affect others?*

| Block | Purpose | What the recruit experiences | Concepts used (DT-1) | Section Commander role | Produces | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1A Your Lead Self Journey** | Why this matters; what Lead Self is in a section | Short framing: leadership starts with how you behave. The four Value Adds, each with a recognisable TAD example. A preview that "by tomorrow you'll have tried something and got evidence on it" | Leadership definition (concept); Value Adds (concept); LDS / Tōtara (reference, brief mention) | Delivers; gives own example of a Value Add in practice | — | Light |
| **1B Who am I?** | Establish identity, i.e. how I see myself and how I intend to come across | Writes a short identity statement: *"How do I want my section to experience me?"* Writes a values commitment: *"How will I show Courage / Commitment / Comradeship / Integrity here?"* | Identity vs Reputation (concept); Ethos & Values (concept, kept); frame of reference (lens, optional prompt) | Explains identity vs reputation in plain words | **V1** identity statement; **M3** values commitment | Light–medium |
| **1C The language of behaviour** | Give the section a shared, observable vocabulary | Generates examples of section behaviours that help or hinder, in their own words. Introduced to Above / Below the Line | Above / Below the Line (lens); trust pillars (lens: "which of these builds or breaks trust?") | Facilitates; adds real examples from TAD | Section help/hinder list (shared) | Medium |
| **1D Section task (observed)** | Generate real behaviour to observe | Section completes a real, low-stakes team task. The strongest candidate is planning the section's own routine using the specified/implied tasks and synchronisation method (workbook pp.47–49). Each recruit is paired with a buddy who observes them using one simple prompt | Intent: specified/implied tasks (activity); followership (lens); team purpose (lens) | Sets the task; observes; notes one behaviour per recruit | Observed behaviour | **Heavy** |
| **1E Feedback: give and receive** | Make Identity vs Reputation experiential (DR-2) | Brief input on how to give feedback (DEB/DESC: what I saw, when, what effect). Buddies exchange one strength and one observation. Section Commander gives each recruit one short observation. Each recruit compares: *what I intended (V1) vs what they saw* | Feedback purposes and DEB/DESC (tool); identity vs reputation (concept, now experienced); trust (lens: how feedback is given) | Models giving feedback first; gives one observation per recruit; manages tone | **V2** peer evidence; **V3** Section Commander evidence; **C2, C3, C4** practised | **Heavy** |
| **1F First insight** | Turn evidence into one thing to try tomorrow | Writes one line: *"Tomorrow, I'll try to ___."* Shares it with their buddy, who will watch for it | Development goal (concept, informal) | Checks each insight is a behaviour, not a trait | First insight (seeds C8) | Light |

### The Identity vs Reputation experience (required by brief §C)

| Step required | Where it happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Consider how they believe they show up | 1B identity statement |
| 2. Behave within a suitable activity | 1D section task |
| 3. Receive observable feedback from another recruit and/or the Section Commander | 1E buddy exchange + one Section Commander observation |
| 4. Compare intention with impact | 1E "what I intended vs what they saw" |
| 5. Identify an initial behavioural insight | 1F first insight |

**The final activity is not designed here.** The section routine task is the leading candidate because it is real (it produces something the section needs), low-stakes, team-based, and already in the workbook. Alternatives are a Phase 3 decision (09).

## 3. Day 2 — Perform, Learn and Develop

*Recruit question: How do I perform? What does the evidence tell me? What will I change?*

| Block | Purpose | What the recruit experiences | Concepts used (DT-1) | Section Commander role | Produces | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2A How do I perform?** | Understand why behaviour changes under pressure | Comfort Zone, with the stretch-zone test ("can I still manage my behaviour, make decisions and notice others?"). Performance = Potential – Interference. Recruits recall a moment from week one so far and locate it in the zones. They name their own early signs of interference | Comfort Zone (lens, recurring); P = P – I (concept); WAFOS, attention tunnelling, memory trap (lens) | Shares a real example of their own interference | **C5** own early signs | Medium |
| **2B Tools that work under pressure** | Give a small, practised toolkit | Practises tactical breathing; one refocus technique (grounding or instructional self-talk); an SA check (*What? So what? What now?*); the circle of concern (what can I control?) | Mental skills (tool, a small subset); SA levels (tool); circle of concern (tool) | Leads practice; uses the tools themselves | **C6** practised | Medium |
| **2C Pressure task: PREPARE → PERFORM → RECOVER** | Test behaviour under pressure, and **reapply the Day 1 insight** | **Prepare:** recall the Day 1 insight; choose one tool. **Perform:** section task under deliberate pressure (time, load, complexity, ambiguity), with a buddy observing *specifically for the insight*. **Recover:** a short debrief | Pressure thread (frame); decisions in time, intent (lenses in task design); team contribution | Designs and runs the pressure; observes; manages safety | **E4, E5**; **V4** buddy evidence on the reapply | **Heavy** |
| **2D What does the evidence tell me?** | Make sense of two days of evidence | Pressure debrief (what happened, what I did, what worked, what next). Then the recruit looks across V1 to V5: *Where do my intention and others' observations agree? Where don't they?* | SSR (tool, simplified); Above / Below the Line (debrief lens); identity vs reputation | Prompts with two or three questions; gives a brief observation where useful | **V5**; **C7** | Medium |
| **2E What will I change?** | Commit to one development priority | Writes the first priority as a behaviour: *"When [situation], I will [behaviour]."* Tags it to a Value Add and a Behaviour Statement. Section Commander checks it is observable | Value Adds and Behaviour Statements (reference); Keep/Stop/Start *(not used here; reserved for the LDP, 03)* | One-to-one or quick check of each priority | **C8**, **M1** first priority (the seed of the LDP) | Medium |
| **2F Section commitment** | Section-level identity | Section agrees how it wants to be seen at march-out, and what will help or hinder that | Culture (lens: what we do here); positive platoon/section culture (activity, kept) | Facilitates; owns it as section leader | **M4** section commitment | Light |
| **2G How this continues** | Hand the loop to the recruit | The loop is *named*: "You've just done EXPERIENCE → FEEDBACK → REFLECT → ADAPT → REAPPLY. This is how you'll keep developing." Buddy pairs confirmed. Part 2 introduced (05). The Section Commander explains when they'll ask about priorities, what happens at Ex One and Ex Two, and where to get help if struggling | Recruit loop (concept); mental-health pathway (reference, duty of care) | Makes the commitment to check in, visibly | **M2**, **N1–N5** | Medium |

### Why the Day 2 pressure task carries the most weight

It is where three things happen in one experience:

1. the **pressure thread** is introduced in practice;
2. the **recruit loop completes its first full turn** (Day 1 insight → Day 2 reapply with evidence);
3. the recruit generates the evidence their **first priority** is built on.

If time is short, protect 1D, 1E and 2C. Concept blocks compress; these do not.

## 4. Deliberate instruction within the two days

*Revised at Phase 2 review.* **No topic earns a standalone lesson by default.** A topic gets deliberate instructional time only where recruits need knowledge, a model or a tool before they can meaningfully practise the behaviour (06 §1.1).

- **Strongest current candidates for explicit instruction (PROVISIONAL):** Value Adds; Identity vs Reputation; Comfort Zone; Feedback/DEB/DESC; P = P – I; mental skills (subset). The concept inputs in blocks 1A, 1B, 1E, 2A and 2B carry these.
- **Open, still under consideration:** Trust, Conflict, Judgement (decision model) and Above/Below the Line may also need short explicit inputs, inside the blocks above or alongside them. **They are not locked out.**
- The mental-health pathway is covered in 2G as duty of care.
