# 06 — Reflection and Evidence System

*Do the workbook's reflection and feedback tools form one development system, or a series of separate activities? And what would a clear evidence trail look like?*

---

## 1. The instruments that exist today

| # | Instrument | Where | Times used | Input | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Strength / Development Area rating** of 5 Behaviour Statements | End of each topic chapter | 11 chapters × 2 pages | Self-perception | 5 ratings |
| R2 | **Keep / Stop / Start** | Each chapter reflection; trust (p.26); both LDPs | 27 | Self-perception | Strategy for one dev area |

## 2. Diagnosis: a series of activities, not yet a system

### 2.1 Flow of evidence today

```
R1/R2 ×11 (chapter reflections) ──▶ ✗ (dead end)
R3 (topic prompts)             ──▶ ✗
R4 SSR ×4                      ──▶ ✗
R5 VA Review One               ──▶ ✗
                                        EX ONE
R6 self + R7 peers ──▶ R8 summary ──▶ R9 LDP-1 ──▶ ✗ (never reviewed)
R5 VA Review Two               ──▶ ✗ (not compared with Review One)
                                        EX TWO
R6 self + R7 peers ──▶ R8 summary ──▶ R9 Final LDP ──▶ ? (no handover)
```

Only one path carries evidence to a plan: **exercise → self + peer feedback → summary → LDP**. It runs twice, and the two runs are not connected to each other.

### 2.2 Specific weaknesses

| # | Weakness | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | **Most reflection is self-perception without evidence.** R1, R2 and R5 ask "am I good at this?" before or apart from any observed event | Ratings are speculative, especially early in training (the workbook itself says so). Recruits learn to fill in forms |
| W2 | **Too many plans.** ~22 chapter K/S/S plans plus 2 LDPs | Dilutes focus. None of the chapter plans is followed up, which teaches recruits that plans are not followed up |
| W3 | **The best evidence instrument (SSR) is disconnected** | Event-anchored self-observation never informs the LDP |
| W4 | **No comparison of identity and reputation.** Self-assessment (R6) and peer summary (R8) sit on adjacent pages; nothing asks "where do they agree or differ?" | The stated purpose of feedback (align identity and reputation, pp.7, 10, 69) is not operationalised |
| W5 | **No reassessment.** LDP-1 is never reviewed at Ex Two | Behavioural change is planned but never evidenced (LO 1.3 weakness) |
| W6 | **Value Add Reviews are not compared** | The change between Review One and Review Two, which is the most meaningful data they could produce, is never captured |
| W7 | **K/S/S applied to strengths** (LDPs) | "Stop" is often meaningless for a strength. The LDP note concedes it ("ok to leave a keep, stop or start blank") |
| W8 | **No evidence field in the LDP** | A priority cannot be traced to where it came from (which feedback, which event) |
| W9 | **No success measure** | Neither LDP asks how the recruit, or anyone else, would know the strategy worked |
| W10 | **Only two perspectives** (self, peers) | No instructor or section commander observation (Decision D9) |
| W11 | **Chapter ratings never aggregate** | 55 ratings produce no picture. Recurring statements are never compared across chapters |

### 2.3 What the system gets right

- **DEB/DESC** is a strong, behaviour-specific format. It forces an observed behaviour, an example and a consequence for individual, group and task. It should be the standard for all behavioural evidence.
- **Peer feedback with verbal disclosure** is powerful at Lead Self and is where reputation data enters.
- **SSR** asks for thoughts, words *and actions*, strategies used, and effectiveness for individual, team *and task*. It is the right structure for a pressure debrief.
- **Value Add tagging** of LDP priorities keeps the plan anchored to the Lead Self standard.
- **Two cycles** gives the structure for reassessment. It only needs connecting.

## 3. The target evidence trail

> **experience → observed behaviour → feedback → reflection → priority → strategy → deliberate practice → reassessment**

| Link | Question it answers | Current instrument | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Experience | What happened? | SSR (event); exercises | SSR too late; exercises good |
| Observed behaviour | What did I actually say and do? | SSR ("thoughts, words and actions"); DEB/DESC "describe the behaviour" | Good formats, not joined |
| Feedback | What did others see? | DEB/DESC peer sheets | Good, 2 points only |
| Reflection | What does the evidence tell me? Where do my view and others' views differ? | Peer summary | **Comparison missing** |
| Priority | What one thing matters most, and why (which Value Add)? | LDP priority + VA tag | Good; **evidence citation missing** |
| Strategy | What exactly will I do, in which situations? | K/S/S + situations | Good for dev areas; awkward for strengths |
| Deliberate practice | When will I try it, and did I? | *None* | **Missing** |
| Reassessment | Did it work? What does new evidence say? | *None* | **Missing** |

## 4. Proposed architecture for reflection, feedback and the LDP

*Conceptual. It defines instruments and flows, not page designs.*

### 4.1 Principle: fewer instruments, used consistently, all feeding one record

Reduce nine instrument types to **five recognisable tools**, each with one job and one learner question, plus one place where evidence accumulates:

| Tool | Job | Learner question | Replaces / builds on | When |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 Baseline** | Record starting identity and self-view | *How do I see myself, and how do I want to be seen by march-out?* | C1 (adds activity), D3 values commitment, R5 Review One, J2 platoon culture end-state | Foundations |
| **T2 Try-it** | Turn a lesson into one behaviour to practise | *Which one behaviour will I try this week, and where?* | R1 + R2 chapter reflections (lighter; one statement, not five; no K/S/S) | After each topic |
| **T3 Pressure debrief** | Capture evidence from a real event | *What happened to me, what did I do, what worked?* | R4 SSR (kept), plus zone check (C2) and mental skill used (M3), tagged to a Value Add | After significant pressured activities, from week one |
| **T4 Feedback** | Capture others' observations | *What did others see?* | R6 + R7 DEB/DESC (kept), R8 summary + **identity/reputation comparison** | After each exercise (and optionally a low-stakes early round) |
| **T5 Plan & review** | Select priority, plan, and later reassess | *What will I do differently, and did it work?* | R9 LDP + **evidence citation, success measure, reassessment** | Post Ex One (plan) → Pre/Post Ex Two (review) → march-out (review + final plan) |

**The Development Record** is a running log (one or two pages, or a tracked table) where the recruit notes each T2 behaviour tried, each T3 debrief, and its Value Add. It is the evidence bank the LDP draws on. It is what turns 30+ scattered reflections into one visible trail.

### 4.2 Flow

```
FOUNDATIONS          EACH WEEK                         EACH EXERCISE CYCLE                           MARCH-OUT
T1 Baseline ─┐       T2 Try-it (per topic) ─┐          ┌─▶ T5 review of previous plan               T5 final review:
 identity    │       T3 Pressure debrief ───┤          │   (did I do it? evidence?)                  • LDP-2 outcomes
 values      │                              ▼          │                                             • values commitment
 VA self-view│              DEVELOPMENT RECORD ────────┤   EXERCISE                                  • platoon culture
 culture     │              (evidence bank, VA-tagged) │   T3 debrief + T4 self & peer feedback      • VA review vs baseline
 end-state   │                              ▲          │   Identity ↔ reputation comparison          T5 final LDP
             └──────────────────────────────┘          └─▶ T5 plan: priority ← evidence              + handover
                                                            strategy → Try-it in next weeks
```

### 4.3 The LDP, strengthened (conceptual fields)

The existing LDP structure (priority strength + priority dev area, each tagged to a Value Add, strategies, situations) is kept. The proposed additions close W5, W7, W8 and W9:

| Field | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Priority (dev area / strength) | Existing | |
| Value Add | Existing | Doctrinal anchor |
| Behaviour Statement(s) it relates to | **Add** | Links the priority to the exact Lead Self standard |
| **Evidence**: where this came from (peer feedback × n, SSR #, self-assessment, instructor) | **Add** | Closes W8. Makes the trail visible |
| **Identity vs reputation**: did I see it myself, or did others? | **Add** | Operationalises C1 |
| Strategy: Keep / Stop / Start (dev area) | Existing | K/S/S keeps its place here, where it works best |
| Strategy for a strength: *Keep and use it more deliberately where?* | **Modify** | Closes W7 |
| Situations to use it | Existing | |
| **What will others see me doing?** | **Add** | Keeps the strategy observable |
| **How will I know it's working?** | **Add** | Closes W9 |
| **Review** (at next cycle): Did I do it? What does new feedback say? Keep, adjust or replace? | **Add** | Closes W5. The reassessment link |

### 4.4 Keep / Stop / Start: recommended scope

| Keep K/S/S in | Replace K/S/S with |
|---|---|
| LDP development-area strategies (2–3 uses) | Chapter reflections → **Try-it** (one behaviour, one situation) |
| SSR Game Plan (Stop/Start, as now) | Trust activity → keep pillar self-assessment; plan goes to Try-it |
| | LDP strengths → "Keep and use deliberately where?" |

Result: about **27 K/S/S instances fall to about 4–6**, each of which leads somewhere.

### 4.5 Value Add Reviews: from repeat to comparison

- Print the Value Add text **once** in short form in the review, with the full text in the reference section.
- Use one review sheet with **columns**: *Baseline (foundations)* / *Pre Ex Two* / *March-out*.
- Add a single reflection prompt at each later column: *What has changed, and what evidence shows it?*

This turns the current duplication (W6) into a visible development trajectory, which is precisely what LO 1.1 asks the recruit to demonstrate.

### 4.6 Topic reflections: from 55 ratings to a selective habit

- Retain Behaviour Statements as the vocabulary. They are doctrinal and should remain visible in every topic.
- Ask the recruit to **pick one** statement per topic to try (T2), and log it in the Development Record.
- If the twin page's purpose was re-rating (Decision D8), provide it as a second column: *rating at lesson / rating before march-out*.

## 6. Constraints and open questions

- **Assessment.** The workbook does not say how the LOs are assessed (PL 1), or whether workbook content is itself assessed. The evidence system must fit the assessment regime (Decision D1).
- **Privacy.** Peer feedback is torn out and handed over. The workbook is "yours to keep". Mental-health self-location is written down. Decide what is private, what is shared and what instructors see (Decisions D10, D12).
- **Recruit load.** Evenings are already scheduled (the RRR routine). T2 and T3 must be short (minutes, not pages).
- **Instructor role.** Whether section commanders contribute observations materially changes the evidence quality (Decision D9).
- **Format.** A Development Record works on paper (a single log table) or digitally. The architecture does not depend on either (Decision D12).
