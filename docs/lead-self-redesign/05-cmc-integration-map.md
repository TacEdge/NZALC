# 05 — Combat Mindset Integration Map

*Where Combat Mindset / Performance Under Pressure concepts intersect the existing Lead Self workbook, **based strictly on the supplied material**. No CMC doctrine is invented here.*

---

## 1. Read this first: status of the sources

| Source | What it is | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **CN**: *Performance Under Pressure and Combat Mindset in the New Zealand Army* | Draft capability note | **Draft**, not endorsed |
| **FP**: *Combat Mindset Framework Proposal* + one-page summary | Paper responding to COMDT ACS direction of 16 Jul 2026. Seeks approval of terminology and a 5-phase programme (Define Sept → Implement late Nov 2026) | **Decisions sought, not yet approved** |
| **WB**: Lead Self Workbook | Already contains NZALC Performance Under Pressure content (P = P – I, mental skills, SSR) | Current, in use |

Four consequences:

1. **The term "Combat Mindset Conditioning" (CMC) does not appear in any supplied source.** The sources use *Combat Mindset*, *Performance Under Pressure (PUP)*, *Combat Mindset Framework* and, separately, the HPC product *Cognitive Conditioning (COGCON)*. This document uses "CMC" only as the tasking's shorthand for this body of material. What the recruit-facing term should be is a decision (D5).

2. **The two drafts define Combat Mindset slightly differently.**
   - CN: "…remain effective and act decisively, **persistently, adaptively** and ethically…"
   - FP: "…remain effective and act decisively and ethically…"

   Neither is approved. Nothing recruit-facing should quote either until one is.

3. **Under FP's recommended Option 1, most Lead Self content is Performance Under Pressure (the enabling capability), not Combat Mindset.** FP's combat-specificity test reserves "combat-specific" for contexts that represent or deliberately simulate "opposition or threat, significant mission or human consequence, and ethical load". See §5.

4. **Timing.** FP Phase 3 (Oct 2026) will "set developmental outcomes from Lead Self through Lead Systems". Redesigning the Lead Self workbook around CM language now risks getting ahead of the framework. It is also an opportunity: both CN (recommendation 7) and FP (Phase 4) call for bounded pilots on existing NZALC activity. See Decision D5.

## 3. The key finding: the Framework already contains PUP behaviours

Integration does **not** require new behavioural standards. The existing Lead Self Behaviour Statements and Value Add text (pp.4–6) already describe performance under pressure:

| Existing doctrinal text | Key Element / Value Add | PUP effect it expresses (CN §4) |
|---|---|---|
| "Stay calm & rational in stressful settings." | IO: Model Self-Awareness and Self Control | Regulate emotion and behaviour |
| "Practice resilience skills to stay calm." | IO | Regulate; mental skills |
| "Maintain physical & mental wellbeing. Seek help if struggling." | IO | Recover |
| "Recognise stressful situations (training) as an opportunity to learn and grow." | IO: Develop the Resilience of Others | Learn from experience |
| "Remain calm, positive and solution focused in the face of stress." | IO: Develop the Resilience of Others | Regulate; retain judgement |
| "Remind others of stress management and resilience techniques." | IO: Develop the Resilience of Others | Cohesion |
| "Maintain Situational Awareness… Get advice when in doubt." | TS | Retain SA |
| "Make decisions in the required time." | TS: Make Decisions | Timely decisions under uncertainty |
| "Adapt to demanding challenges." | DT | Adapt |
| "Adapt behaviour to support changes." / "Remain positive during change." | DPC: Embrace Change | Adapt |
| "They improve the quality of their decisions by **remaining calm and increasing their situational awareness**… use NZDF Values to guide their judgement and decisions, particularly in times of **uncertainty and ambiguity**." | **Apply Good Judgement** (Value Add) | Retain judgement and SA under pressure |
| "They **remain calm and solution-focused when faced with tension or conflict**… support their team-mates to maintain resilience and wellbeing." | **Support Others** (Value Add) | Regulate; cohesion |
| "They engage with whānau and trusted support networks… to help maintain resilience and wellbeing." | **Pursue Excellence** (Value Add) | Recover |
| Courage: "Taking action despite your fears… moral and physical strength to do what is right, even in the face of adversity." | E&V | CN combat demand: "continue acting despite fear"; CM definition: "act… ethically" |

**Implication.** CMC/PUP can be integrated as the *how* behind behaviours the Lead Self standard already requires. That is doctrinally safe: nothing is added to the standard, and the recruit's evidence stays tagged to existing Value Adds and Behaviour Statements.

## 5. The combat-specificity question for recruit training

The course context is explicitly combat-focused: "To produce a combat focused Band One soldier…" (p.ii). Applying FP's test (Option 1, recommended but not approved):

| Recruit training context | Opposition / threat | Significant consequence | Ethical load | Classification under FP test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Barracks routine, inspections, lessons | ✗ | Low–moderate | Low | Enabling capability (PUP) |
| Physical training, assessments | ✗ | Moderate (course consequence) | Low | Enabling capability (PUP) |
| Field exercises (Ex One, Ex Two) | Possibly simulated | Possibly | Possibly | **Depends on exercise design.** Could qualify as combat-specific if it deliberately simulates all three |
| Values-based dilemmas (speaking up, peer pressure) | ✗ | Moderate | **High** | Enabling capability. Ethical load alone does not meet the test |

**Implication.** If Option 1 is approved, labelling Lead Self workbook content wholesale as "Combat Mindset" would be doctrinally inaccurate. The defensible position is:

- **Lead Self develops Performance Under Pressure**, consistent with CN's "Lead Self: perform under pressure";
- **Combat Mindset is expressed** where exercises deliberately simulate combat demands;
- **Ethos & Values provides the ethical anchor** that CM requires.

If COMDT ACS selects Option 2 (CM as umbrella), CM language can be used throughout. **This is why D5 must be resolved first, or the design must allow terminology to be swapped late.**

### Integration principle

CN recommends that Army "does not immediately create another standalone Combat Mindset course or duplicate existing content". FP states that existing products contribute to the capability but "none constitutes the framework in isolation". Both support integrating PUP/CM as a **thread** through the Lead Self journey rather than adding a standalone chapter. In practice the thread would be:

- **one model** (P = P – I), taught early;
- **one self-check** (comfort-zone / noticing), used repeatedly;
- **a small personal set of mental skills**, practised deliberately;
- **one debrief tool** (SSR), used after every significant pressure event;
- all **tagged to existing Behaviour Statements and Value Adds**, so no new standard is created.

## 7. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Workbook adopts CM terminology before the Framework is approved and later has to be reprinted | Resolve D5. Design with swappable terminology; default to PUP language already in NZALC use |
| "Combat Mindset" read by recruits as aggression or toughness | Use CN's framing explicitly: "controlled, disciplined and effective" |
| Inventing content to fill gaps (WIN, Red/Blue Head, PPR detail) | Obtain the NZALC PUP Workbook and any WIN source before Phase 2 |
| Mental health content reframed as performance | Keep M4 as a distinct duty-of-care item with a help-seeking pathway |
| Duplication with HPC COGCON | FP Phase 2 will assess COGCON. Do not reference it in Lead Self until that assessment reports |
