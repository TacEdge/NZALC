# 04 — Proposed Development Architecture (Conceptual)

*This document tests the existing content against a proposed developmental spine and outlines architecture options. It is deliberately conceptual. **It does not design pages, chapters or layouts**, and it changes no doctrine.*

---

## 1. The spine under test

> **ORIENTATE → UNDERSTAND SELF → LEARN → PRACTICE → PRESSURE → APPLY → REFLECT → ADAPT → REINFORCE**

It is treated here as a **learning architecture**: the sequence of developmental functions a recruit should pass through. It is not nine chapters.

Each step is given a working definition and a single learner question, so that the steps can be told apart in design:

| Step | Developmental function | Learner question | Primary LO |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ORIENTATE** | Understand why Lead Self matters, what the standard is and how the development system works | *Why does this matter, and how does this work?* | 1.2 |
| **UNDERSTAND SELF** | Establish a baseline: identity, values, tendencies, current self-view against the Value Adds | *Who am I now, and how do I want to be seen?* | 1.1 |
| **LEARN** | Acquire a concept, model or tool | *What does this mean?* | 1.1, 1.2 |
| **PRACTICE** | Deliberately try the behaviour in low-stakes conditions | *Try it: what happened when I did?* | 1.2 |
| **PRESSURE** | Experience demanding conditions and notice one's own responses | *What happened to me?* | 1.1 |
| **APPLY** | Use the chosen behaviour in real training, under real conditions | *What did I actually do?* | 1.2 |
| **REFLECT** | Examine evidence from self, peers (and, if decided, instructors) | *What does the evidence tell me?* | 1.1 |
| **ADAPT** | Select a priority and a strategy; commit to specific situations | *What will I do differently, and when?* | 1.3 |
| **REINFORCE** | Reassess, consolidate and carry the behaviour forward | *What behaviour am I carrying forward?* | 1.3 |

## 2. Relationship to existing NZALC methodologies

### 2.1 LEARN → PRACTICE → PRESSURE → APPLY → REINFORCE

The nine-step spine is the NZALC five-step methodology with **four additional functions**:

```
        ORIENTATE → UNDERSTAND SELF → LEARN → PRACTICE → PRESSURE → APPLY → REFLECT → ADAPT → REINFORCE
NZALC:                                LEARN → PRACTICE → PRESSURE → APPLY ─────────────────→ REINFORCE
                 └────── front end ──────┘                                 └─ explicit loop ─┘
```

- **Front end (ORIENTATE, UNDERSTAND SELF).** These give the recruit a reason and a baseline. Both matter more at Lead Self than at higher levels: recruits arrive with no LDS context, and LO 1.1 is about self-awareness.
- **REFLECT and ADAPT.** In the five-step model these are implicit within REINFORCE. The Lead Self LOs *assess* them directly (LO 1.1 self-awareness, LO 1.3 LDP), so making them explicit is justified for this course.

**Finding.** The two models are compatible. The nine-step spine is best understood as the Lead Self **expansion** of the NZALC methodology, not a competitor. Recommendation: keep the five-step as the recognisable NZALC training language, and use the nine-step as the internal design scaffold for this workbook (Decision D3).

### 2.2 Prepare – Perform – Recover

CN and FP identify **Prepare–Perform–Recover** as existing NZALC doctrine used "at both daily and course level" in the ELDA. It does not currently appear in the Lead Self workbook (its nearest relative is "Review & Recover", p.59). It maps naturally onto the loop portion of the spine:

| Prepare–Perform–Recover | Spine |
|---|---|
| **Prepare** | LEARN, PRACTICE (and ADAPT from the previous cycle) |
| **Perform** | PRESSURE, APPLY |
| **Recover** | REFLECT, ADAPT |

This is relevant to CMC integration (doc 05). If Prepare–Perform–Recover is approved for Lead Self use, it offers a short, recruit-friendly frame for the repeating loop, and it is already NZALC-owned.

### 2.3 Terminology collisions to resolve

| Term | Collision | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **ORIENTATE** | The LDS already uses *Orientate – Build – Advance* (p.3), and recruit training **is** the Orientate phase ("90 days prior – 6 months"). | Recruits and instructors conflate a workbook step with an LDS career phase. Suggest a different label for the design step (e.g. *Foundations*, *Why & How*). |
| **PRESSURE vs APPLY** | In recruit training these usually co-occur (on exercise you are under pressure *and* applying behaviour). | Hard for learners to separate. Keep the distinction for designers (noticing one's responses vs deliberately enacting a behaviour); consider combining them in learner-facing language. |
| **REFLECT vs "Lesson Reflection"** | Current "Lesson Reflection" pages are mostly self-rating and planning, not evidence examination. | Carrying the old label forward carries the old habit forward. |

### Distribution findings

| Step | Current weight | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| ORIENTATE | Heavy on doctrine, nothing on the system | Recruits learn the Framework but not how the workbook will develop them |
| UNDERSTAND SELF | Present but **scattered** (C1, C2, D3, H1, M4, N4) | Baseline material spread across four chapters and never consolidated into a baseline |
| LEARN | **Heaviest step** | Sound. Needs register simplification, not more content |
| PRACTICE | **Thin**: K3, E2, C6 and not much else | The biggest content gap |
| PRESSURE | Mostly **outside the workbook** (exercises, daily training) | The workbook has no instrument for noticing responses under pressure until SSR in Ch 12 |
| APPLY | Outside the workbook | Nothing captures "what I actually did" except post-exercise self-assessment |
| REFLECT | Heavy but **fragmented** | Many instruments, not joined up (doc 06) |
| ADAPT | Heavy (22 K/S/S + 2 LDPs) | Too many plans, too few carried forward |
| REINFORCE | **Nearly absent** | No reassessment of LDP-1, no revisit of commitments, no handover |

**The existing workbook is front-loaded onto LEARN and back-loaded onto ADAPT, with little in between and almost nothing after.** The redesign opportunity is mainly in PRACTICE, PRESSURE-noticing and REINFORCE, which need tools more than content.

## 4. The spine is a loop, not a line

Section 3 shows the spine behaves differently at two scales:

```
COURSE ARC (once)
  Foundations ──▶ [ Development loop, repeated ] ──▶ Carry forward
  (ORIENTATE,                                         (REINFORCE:
   UNDERSTAND SELF)                                    reassess, consolidate,
                                                       hand over)

DEVELOPMENT LOOP (repeats: per topic week, per pressured activity, per exercise)

        ┌──────────── LEARN ───────────┐
        │                              ▼
      ADAPT                        PRACTICE
        ▲                              │
        │                              ▼
     REFLECT ◀──── APPLY ◀──────── PRESSURE
```

- **Topic lessons** mainly feed LEARN → PRACTICE.
- **Daily training and pressured activities** are PRESSURE → APPLY → REFLECT (light, SSR-style).
- **The two exercises** are the full loop at high intensity, with peer feedback in REFLECT and a formal LDP in ADAPT.
- **Each loop should start by checking the previous ADAPT.** At present, nothing checks it.

This matches the Prepare–Perform–Recover pattern CN describes as operating "at both daily and course level".

## 5. Architecture options

Three ways the existing content could be organised on this spine. Each keeps all doctrinal content and all KEEP/MODIFY components from doc 02.

### Option A — Topic-led (current order, loop added)

Keep the 11-topic sequence. Add a light development loop to each topic. Add a reassessment step in each exercise cycle and a carry-forward at march-out.

- **For:** least disruption; instructors' lesson plans stay valid.
- **Against:** keeps pressure material late; Value Adds stay implicit; the book still reads as eleven parallel topics.

### Option B — Value Add-led

Group topics under the four Value Adds, with Ethos & Values as the foundation beneath all four (consistent with the Tōtara roots):

| Value Add | Topics (existing) |
|---|---|
| *Foundation:* Ethos & Values | Mission, Ethos, Values; Above/Below the Line |
| **Support Others** | Trust; Conflict; Team Development; Culture |
| **Follow Leader's Intent** | Intent; Functional leadership & Followership |
| **Apply Good Judgement** | Judgement & Decisions (SA, WAFOS, decisions, risk) |
| **Pursue Excellence** | Lead Self Development (feedback); Change; Resilience & mental skills |

- **For:** every page speaks the language of LO 1.2 and the LDP. Recruits accumulate evidence *by Value Add* from day one, so LDP tagging becomes trivial.
- **Against:** Support Others is heavy (4 topics) against Apply Good Judgement (1). It may not match the TAD delivery sequence. Needs instructor lesson re-sequencing or cross-referencing.

### Option C — Cycle-led (recommended for consideration)

Structure the course as **Foundations → development cycles → Carry forward**. The exercise cycles form the backbone, and topics are slotted in as LEARN/PRACTICE inputs to the cycle they precede. Each topic is **tagged** to its Value Add, which takes the best of Option B without forcing the re-grouping.

```
FOUNDATIONS                  CYCLE 1                          CYCLE 2                          CARRY FORWARD
Why & how the system works   Topics (tagged to VA)            Topics (tagged to VA)            Reassess LDP-2
Identity statement           + weekly pressure debriefs       + weekly pressure debriefs       Revisit values & culture
Values commitment            → Prepare for Ex One             → Check LDP-1                    commitments
Comfort zone, P = P – I      → EX ONE                         → Prepare for Ex Two             Final LDP + handover
2–3 mental skills + SSR      → Self/peer feedback             → EX TWO
Feedback basics              → Evidence review                → Self/peer feedback
Baseline VA self-review      → LDP-1                          → Evidence review
                                                              → VA review vs baseline
```

- **For:** puts pressure tools at the front, where recruits need them. Makes the exercises the laboratory the workbook is built around ("the activity is the laboratory, not the product", CN §6). Builds in reassessment and carry-forward. Keeps topic lessons intact as modules.
- **Against:** needs the TAD timetable to place topics accurately (Decision D2). It is a bigger change for instructors than Option A.

**Recommendation.** Decide between A and C at the review. B's main benefit (Value Add tagging) can be added to either. The decision depends on how much freedom NZALC has to re-sequence delivery (Decision D4).

---

## 6. Design constraints carried forward

Whatever option is chosen, the architecture must:

1. Keep LO 1.1–1.3 and the course aim verbatim.
2. Reproduce doctrinal content (Framework, Value Adds, Behaviour Statements, Values, te reo) without alteration.
3. Treat the two exercises and their feedback cycle as the load-bearing structure. They are what works.
4. Give each step a **distinct** learner experience (section 1 questions), so reflection stops feeling like one repeated form.
5. Produce a continuous evidence trail ending in the final LDP (doc 06).
6. Stay usable by an 18-year-old: plain language, few instruments, each used repeatedly and recognisably.
