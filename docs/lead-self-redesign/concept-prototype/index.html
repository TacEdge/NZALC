Day 1Why this matters

You already lead. The question is how.

In a section, everyone influences everyone. How you turn up when you're tired, behind, or wrong changes how the section performs.

That influence is leadership at the Lead Self level. It doesn't need a rank. It needs you to see your own behaviour clearly and to change it on purpose.

Leadership is influencing, motivating and enabling others to contribute to the effectiveness and success of the New Zealand Defence Force, in an ethical manner.

NZDDP 0.06, para 1.11 · Workbook p.1

Over the next two days you will

Do a real task while someone watches how you turn up.

Get evidence from another person about what they saw.

Pick one thing to do differently, and try it under pressure.

By tomorrow night you'll have a method you can keep using without us.

Section Commander at this moment

  • Delivers this framing in their own words. One example of a time their own behaviour changed how a section went.
  • Sets the expectation: "Over these two days I'll watch, I'll tell you one thing I saw, and I'll ask you about it for the rest of the course."

Day 1Lead Self expectations

Four ways you add value to the section

These are the Lead Self Value Adds. The sentence under each is from the NZDF Leadership Framework. The list is what it looks like in a section, in the Framework's own words.

Follow Leader's Intent

Whai Hau Tū

Lead Self Leaders deliver the right outcomes efficiently and on time by acting in accordance with their leaders intent.

Looks like

  • Check you and others understand the purpose of your tasks.
  • Prioritise and manage your own time and resources to ensure work is completed efficiently and on time.
  • Accept accountability for your actions, outputs and behaviours.

Apply Good Judgement

Taituara

They improve the quality of their decisions by remaining calm and increasing their situational awareness.

Looks like

  • Pay attention to what's happening around you.
  • Check you and others understand the situation. Get advice when in doubt.
  • Make decisions in the required time.

Support Others

Whakaaro Nui

They are predictable, consistent and fair, regularly reflecting on their behaviour to understand and manage the impact they have on those around them.

Looks like

  • Actively share information and collaborate. Work towards solutions with others.
  • Admit mistakes and ask for help when needed.
  • Speak up against behaviour that does not meet NZDF Ethos & Values.

Pursue Excellence

Whai Hiranga

They dedicate time and effort to their own development, are proactive in keeping up to date, and are curious and open to new ideas and initiatives.

Looks like

  • Use mistakes as opportunities to learn.
  • Regularly reflect on your behaviour & performance to become self-aware.
  • Practice resilience skills to stay calm.

Value Adds: Workbook p.4 · Behaviour Statements: Workbook pp.5–6 (verbatim)

Full Value Add text (reference)

Follow Leader's Intent. Lead Self Leaders deliver the right outcomes efficiently and on time by acting in accordance with their leaders intent. They ensure they clearly understand the intent and purpose of their team, role and tasks, by asking questions of their peers and Team Leader. Tasks are completed effectively by prioritising effort, managing time, making timely decisions and coordinating with peers. They demonstrate accountability for the results of their work and behaviour by following-through on commitments and openly discussing their mahi, including successes and failures, and ideas for improvement. Self Leaders actively seek feedback to improve, and consider diverse perspectives.

Apply Good Judgement. Lead Self Leaders apply good judgement to make effective decisions that align with and deliver on their team's mission. They improve the quality of their decisions by remaining calm and increasing their situational awareness. They support team-mates with this too. Self Leaders use logic, experience, others' perspectives and sound practical judgment when considering options. They understand their leader's intent and that their tasks and responsibilities contribute to a larger 'whole', no matter how mundane they are. Self Leaders use NZDF Values to guide their judgement and decisions, particularly in times of uncertainty and ambiguity.

Support Others. All NZDF leaders are members of a team and always have responsibility to be an effective team member; to support others, build trust, cohesion and team effectiveness. Self Leaders consistently align their behaviour with NZDF values. They are predictable, consistent and fair, regularly reflecting on their behaviour to understand and manage the impact they have on those around them. They connect and collaborate inclusively with others, regardless of background. Self Leaders are curious and ask questions to understand different perspectives on issues the team is facing. They remain calm and solution-focused when faced with tension or conflict, and support healthy debate by being open and respectful. Self Leaders speak up when they observe behaviours that do not meet the NZDF Values. They support their team-mates to maintain resilience and wellbeing.

Pursue Excellence. Lead Self Leaders are key to delivering high quality outcomes 'on the ground'. To do this well, they must focus on becoming an expert in all aspects of the technical and professional skills required of them, striving to be the best they can be. They dedicate time and effort to their own development, are proactive in keeping up to date, and are curious and open to new ideas and initiatives. They are willing to challenge the status quo and take appropriate risks when trialling new methods.They seek coaching, and provide honest feedback about their own and others' performance and impact on the team. They engage with whanau and trusted support networks in and outside NZDF to help maintain resilience and wellbeing. Self Leaders embrace change in a positive manner and help others to do so too.

How do I want my section to experience me?

This is your identity: how you see you. Tomorrow you'll put it next to your reputation: how others see you. Keep it short and honest.

Why write this down

Identity – how you see you. Reputation – how others see you.

Workbook p.7

Most people's identity and reputation don't match perfectly. The gap is where development happens. You can't see the gap on your own. That's what the next two days are for.

Day 1Experience

Now do something real while someone watches

Your section plans tonight's routine. It has to work. While you do it, your buddy watches one thing: what you actually say and do.

The task

Plan the section's evening routine so nobody is ever waiting.

  • List what each person has to get done and how long it takes.
  • Find the bottlenecks (showers, irons, boot brushes).
  • Synchronise so the section finishes together, on time.

Based on Workbook pp.47–49 · Specified and implied tasks; synchronising routine

Your buddy's job

Watch. Don't help. Notice one thing that helped and one thing that got in the way.

Write exactly what you saw, and when. Not what kind of person they are.

You'll be watched too. That's the deal.

Nothing here is graded. It's the first time you get evidence.

Section Commander at this moment

  • Sets the task with a real constraint (tonight's timings). Pairs buddies.
  • Observes. Notes one specific behaviour per recruit, with the moment it happened. That's the only thing they write today.
  • Does not intervene in the planning unless safety requires it.
