# 03 — The Current Development Journey

*What a recruit experiences with this workbook from start of training to march-out, and where the chain from knowledge to behaviour change holds or breaks.*

> **Information gap.** The workbook gives no timetable. Its only time anchors are "Exercise One", "Exercise Two" and "march-out". It does not say when each topic lesson falls relative to the exercises. The journey below follows workbook order, which is presumably close to delivery order. **The TAD Lead Self delivery schedule is needed to confirm it** (Decision D2).

In practice the exercise cycles are probably interleaved with the topic lessons, not after them. Chapter 13 is a *container* for activities spread across the course, which the workbook signals only through page cross-references on p.68.

**The only continuous evidence chain in the workbook is: exercise → self/peer feedback → peer summary → LDP.** It runs twice. Everything else is a dead end.

## 2. The knowledge-to-behaviour chain: where it holds and where it breaks

Target chain:

> **knowledge → practice → experience → feedback → reflection → behavioural change**

| Link | Current state | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge** | Well covered. Eleven topics, sound models, largely doctrinal. Some text is at an academic register (SA levels, Schein, strategic self-awareness). | **Strong** |
| **Knowledge → Practice** | Mostly absent. Topic activities ask recruits to *recall past examples* ("my example of…") rather than *try something*. The genuine practice activities are the RRR routine (p.49), perhaps the platoon culture plan (p.44), and the trust pillar plan (p.26). Conflict conversations (p.30) and mental skills (p.59) are skills taught without practice. | **Weak** |
| **Practice → Experience (under pressure)** | Pressure is the defining condition of recruit training, but the workbook treats it as **a topic at the end** (Ch 12), not as the constant environment. The comfort zone (p.8) is introduced and dropped. SSRs appear only after all topic lessons. The two exercises are the only formally designated pressure experiences. | **Weak** |
| **Experience → Feedback** | Strong at the two exercises: structured, peer-based, DEB/DESC, disclosed verbally. Absent between them: no mechanism for everyday feedback, and no instructor or section commander observations anywhere. | **Strong at 2 points, absent elsewhere** |
| **Feedback → Reflection** | The peer summary asks recruits to prioritise feedback against the Value Adds. That is a real reflection step. It does not ask them to compare feedback (reputation) against their self-assessment (identity), even though that comparison is the stated point of feedback (pp.7, 10, 69). | **Moderate** |
| **Reflection → Behavioural change** | LDPs specify strategies and situations. But there is **no reassessment**: Exercise Two feedback is never compared with LDP-1 goals. The recruit sets a mid-course plan and nothing asks whether they did it. Behaviour change is intended but never evidenced. | **Weak** |
| **Behavioural change → Reinforcement / transfer** | The final LDP looks forward to the next phase. There is no handover and no revisit of the values commitment (p.17) or platoon culture end-state (p.44), both of which were written with march-out in mind. | **Weak** |

## 4. Principal gaps (ranked)

1. **No reassessment loop.** LDP-1 is never reviewed. Without "did I do what I planned, and what does the evidence say?", the workbook produces *plans*, not *demonstrated change*. This matters for all three LOs, most of all LO 1.3.

2. **Reflection is fragmented and does not accumulate.** Eleven chapter reflections, four SSRs and two Value Add Reviews produce substantial self-knowledge that never reaches the LDP. The LDP is built on two post-exercise feedback rounds only.

3. **Pressure is a topic, not the environment.** Comfort zone (p.8) and the performance/mental-skills material (pp.58–59) bracket the book. Recruits get the tools for managing pressure after most of their pressure exposure has happened.

4. **Identity vs Reputation is not operationalised.** The stated purpose of feedback is to align identity and reputation. The workbook never records an identity statement to compare feedback against, and never asks for the comparison.

5. **Little deliberate practice.** Most activities ask for recollection or opinion. Skills (conflict conversations, mental skills, feedback) are described but not rehearsed. The RRR routine shows the workbook can do practice well.

6. **Value Adds are the standard but not the spine.** LO 1.2 is "Add Value" and the LDP is Value Add-tagged, yet topic chapters never state which Value Add they build. The recruit has to do that synthesis alone at the last moment.

7. **Only self and peer perspectives.** No instructor or section commander observation appears anywhere. Peer feedback from fellow recruits in the orientation phase is valuable but limited (Decision D9).

8. **No transfer mechanism.** The final LDP is "yours to keep", with no handover to the next phase or unit.

9. **Duplication hides the system.** About half of the pages are reflection templates, many of them identical. The recruit sees a repetitive book rather than a deliberate progression.

## 5. What already works (and must survive the redesign)

- **The exercise feedback cycle**: self → peers → disclosure → summary → plan. This is the core of LO 1.1 and 1.3, and it is structurally sound.
- **DEB/DESC format** with relatable worked examples (pp.11, 70).
- **Systematic Self Reflection**: event-anchored, with thoughts, words and actions, strategies used, and effectiveness for individual, team and task. The best raw evidence instrument in the book.
- **Topic-specific applied activities**: trust pillars (p.26), platoon culture (p.44), specified/implied tasks (p.47), RRR routine (p.49), circle of concern (p.54).
- **Doctrinal fidelity.** The Leadership Framework, Value Adds and Behaviour Statements are reproduced faithfully.
- **Recruit-context examples** (bed-making, platoon fatigues, forming up) that make abstract behaviours concrete.
